## Walk-through results (authored desk analysis, not participant testing)

### SCN-001 — AI candidate, evidence first

LC-001: business owner and service problem exist; personal-text uncertainty requires specialist input before data use. LC-002: 18% transfer count is not error rate; category ambiguity and capacity compete with language interpretation as causes. LC-003: compare guidance/rules repair, assisted routing and no change. LC-004: independent labels, avoidable work, review cost and permitted data access are missing. LC-005: do not rank deployment value from unknown net savings. LC-006 proposed disposition: MORE EVIDENCE REQUIRED for a bounded comparison, not AI implementation approval.

Reversal condition: if ambiguous cases remain material after category repair and authorised evidence demonstrates useful assistance net of checking/rework, recommend a separately authorised next investigation stage (PROCEED). If rules resolve the material pain, prefer NON-AI ROUTE. If service capacity dominates, refine the problem. No such new evidence has actually been collected.

### SCN-002 — Conventional solution direction

LC-001: clarify “approve” versus “route”; finance authority is indispensable. LC-002: existing routing table and approver absences explain plausible delay; overlapping counts cannot be added. LC-003: completeness checks/delegation/process repair fit better than AI approval under supplied premises. LC-004: finance must confirm delegation/control, engineering assess effort and operational owner measure returns/waits. LC-005: prefer a bounded conventional change assessment, with no claimed ROI. LC-006 proposed disposition: NON-AI ROUTE to the purchasing/process owner, conditional on required authority, rather than a development task here.

Reversal condition: if exceptions are frequent, materially costly and require interpretation, reassess assistive options. If delegation itself is prohibited or change cost exceeds benefit, refine/park rather than force automation. A non-AI route is successful BA analysis, not product failure; it also challenges whether a new tool is necessary.

### SCN-003 — Assurance hold and alternate problem framing

LC-001: early governance already exists; consequential rejection and sensitive context trigger referral to authorised specialists. LC-002: queue stock does not identify the bottleneck; outcome labels may encode historic bias. LC-003: compare administrative completeness/queue improvements with rejection automation. LC-004: no specialist permission, DPIA determination, fairness assessment or meaningful oversight evidence exists in the case. LC-005: do not rank blocked rejection automation using potential throughput. LC-006 proposed disposition: ESCALATE GOVERNANCE and hold the affected proposal. Safe desk examination of administrative alternatives can continue within scope.

Reversal condition: if required assurance cannot be met, recommend STOP for rejection automation. If a narrower administrative option can be responsibly justified, return to options/assurance, then seek explicit authority. No legal conclusion, approval or safeguard effectiveness is asserted. High risk does not prove the business need is invalid.

### SCN-004 — Park before unnecessary analysis

LC-001: request lacks an actionable decision and operational owner. LC-002 limited clarification finds only a small self-selected sample and unused summary; this does not justify full option/feasibility work. LC-003..005 deferred proportionately rather than fabricated as completed. LC-006 proposed disposition: PARK; sponsor to revisit when a named decision owner supplies a recurring decision, intended action and credible evidence need. Recommend STOP if the sponsor confirms no useful decision or withdraws the proposition; no arbitrary deadline invented.

Reversal condition: a credible decision/use could justify renewed discovery, including a non-AI summary/action meeting. The initial weak request is not proof sentiment analysis is universally useless.
