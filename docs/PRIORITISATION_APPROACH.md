# Opportunity Prioritisation Approach

Status: DRAFT
Owner: Namit Singh
Phase: 2
Basis: Phase 1 F-A2 and F-E1, EV-009/015/016/017 in research/sources/PHASE1_SOURCES.md; LC-004..006; four synthetic scenarios.

Prioritisation already exists in adjacent offerings. A simple score is not claimed as innovation. This proposal makes evidence strength, blockers, trade-offs and decision ownership visible. It is business decision practice, not a software algorithm or detailed product requirement.

## Three different uses of information

| Dimension | Primary use | Decision treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Required data/processing permission and material assurance | HARD GATE for affected action | Passed / unresolved / blocked with authorised evidence. Unknown cannot pass; high value cannot offset a blocker. Specialist determines applicable conditions. |
| Named business/operational decision owner | HARD GATE for progression commitment | No owner: refine/park. BA can still clarify the request. |
| Funding or resource authority needed for proposed action | HARD GATE for that commitment | Approval to analyse is not approval to spend/build. |
| Business value and harm | DECISION INPUT; RANKING INPUT when comparable | Outcome chain, beneficiaries, range and costs; avoid combining incomparable financial and non-financial benefit. |
| Evidence strength | DECISION INPUT | Stipulated, observed, inferred and unknown distinguished; insufficient evidence triggers bounded validation, not an arbitrary numeric penalty. |
| Strategic alignment | DECISION / RANKING INPUT | Sponsor confirms intended objective and competing priorities; mandatory obligations are handled explicitly rather than hidden in weights. |
| Feasibility and data readiness | DECISION INPUT; may expose HARD GATE | Technical authority states limits; unavailable authorised data can block proposed assessment. Plausible does not mean demonstrated. |
| Delivery effort / opportunity cost | RANKING INPUT when estimates comparable | Include analysis, change, support, assurance and ongoing operation; use ranges and confidence. |
| Governance/residual risk | HARD GATE where assurance requires; otherwise DECISION INPUT | Competent authority determines disposition. Never trade a legal prohibition against a score. |
| Adoption/change complexity | DECISION / RANKING INPUT | Operational owner, training, exceptions, workload and actual usage measurement; missing ownership can block commitment. |

## Procedure

1. State the next action being compared (evidence collection, process refinement or later delivery); do not compare an investigation budget with a fully costed deployment as if equivalent.
2. Apply relevant hard gates. Record blocked/unresolved items separately; define the work that could resolve them.
3. Build a short comparison of eligible actions using consistent units/periods and explicit confidence. Missing estimates stay unknown.
4. Compare value, effort, strategy, adoption and dependency trade-offs. Use dominance only where one option is no worse on all material dimensions under comparable evidence. Otherwise retain human judgement and dissent.
5. Test whether a plausible change in effort, realised benefit or capacity changes the preference. If it does, prioritise obtaining that evidence or state a conditional choice.
6. BA recommends; sponsor decides portfolio priority; separate finance, governance and technical authority remains intact. Record rationale, alternative, conditions and revisit trigger. No automatic weighted total.

## Illustrative four-case comparison (not a real portfolio ranking)

| Case | Gate/evidence position | Proportionate next action | Why not declare a winner now? |
|---|---|---|---|
| SCN-001 | Routing problem plausible; avoidable work and data permission unclear | MORE EVIDENCE REQUIRED: compare category/rules repair with assisted ambiguous routing | Unknown checking burden/data readiness may reverse expected benefit |
| SCN-002 | Explicit rules and absence delays favour conventional change; policy authority must confirm | Recommend NON-AI ROUTE for bounded process/rules assessment | No implemented change or cost estimate; exception complexity could alter route |
| SCN-003 | Consequential automation assurance unresolved | ESCALATE GOVERNANCE; hold rejection automation, examine administrative alternative separately | Potential harm cannot be offset by backlog size or hypothetical ROI |
| SCN-004 | No decision owner/use or credible value | PARK with named sponsor and defined evidence trigger; recommend STOP if no useful proposition emerges | Visibility might become useful if a real recurring decision is identified |

No claim is made that SCN-002 is always more important than SCN-001. Urgency, effort, available authority and service harm can change allocation. SCN-003's assurance work could itself be urgent even while the proposed automation is blocked.

## Deterministic illustration and sensitivity

SCN-001/E1: 360 / 2,000 = 18% transfers in the stipulated four-week period, not 18% wrong routes. SCN-001/E2: 18 / 100 = 18% transfers in the authored sample; this does not prove sample representativeness or cause shares in the population.

For illustration only, if 100 baseline routing hours have 30% gross avoidable effort, and additional review/rework is 20 hours, net released capacity = 100 × 0.30 − 20 = 10 hours per four weeks. With 10% gross avoidable effort, net = 100 × 0.10 − 20 = −10 hours. The 30%, 10% and 20 hours are sensitivity assumptions, not forecasts, measured results or benefits in the case. Do not annualise or monetise without a supported basis; released capacity is not automatically cash saving. Unknown implementation/operating costs mean no ROI conclusion.

## Human review focus

Challenge whether the comparison targets the actual bottleneck, whether evidence is selective, who bears harm, whether extra BA process costs exceed benefit, and whether an existing tool or simple process change suffices. Maintain the option to stop the product hypothesis itself; scenario success cannot prove demand for a new application.
