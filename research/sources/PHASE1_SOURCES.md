## Purpose

### EV-001 — Gartner: 30% of GenAI projects abandoned after PoC
- Organisation/author: Gartner (Rita Sallam, Distinguished VP Analyst)
- Type: ANALYST
- URL: https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-29-gartner-predicts-30-percent-of-generative-ai-projects-will-be-abandoned-after-proof-of-concept-by-end-of-2025
- Published: 2024-07-29 | Accessed: 2026-09-11
- Key finding: Gartner predicts at least 30% of generative AI projects will be abandoned after proof of concept by end of 2025. Cited causes: poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs, unclear business value. Reported deployment cost range for GenAI business-model transformation: $5M–$20M.
- Relevance: Direct evidence that AI demand is poorly qualified before investment.
- Limitations: A prediction, not a measured outcome; press-release summary of paywalled research.

### EV-002 — MIT NANDA: The GenAI Divide (State of AI in Business 2025)
- Organisation/author: MIT / Project NANDA initiative (reported via Fortune)
- Type: INDUSTRY (academic-affiliated report; accessed via secondary coverage)
- URL: https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo
- Published: 2025-08-18 | Accessed: 2026-09-11
- Key finding: ~95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots fail to deliver measurable P&L impact. Failure attributed to organisational integration and learning gaps, not model quality; misaligned budgets; central-lab control. Reported: specialised vendor solutions succeed ~67% of the time vs internal builds roughly one-third as often. Basis: 150 interviews, 350-person survey, ~300 public deployments.
- Relevance: Strong evidence for the deployment-vs-value gap and that discovery/adoption discipline (not just tooling) matters.
- Limitations: Headline figure accessed via secondary coverage; "failure" defined as no rapid P&L impact; methodology not independently audited here.

### EV-003 — McKinsey: The state of AI in early 2024
- Organisation/author: McKinsey & Company (QuantumBlack)
- Type: ANALYST/INDUSTRY (survey)
- URL: https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-2024
- Published: 2024-05-30 | Accessed: 2026-09-11
- Key finding: 65% of organisations regularly use gen AI in ≥1 function; ~72% report AI adoption overall. Value capture is early — as reported, only 46 of 876 respondents attribute >10% of EBIT to gen AI. Top scaling barrier is data governance/integration; only ~18% report enterprise-wide responsible-AI governance.
- Relevance: Evidence that adoption is high but measured value and governance maturity lag.
- Limitations: Self-reported survey; figures shift across annual waves.

### EV-004 — ICO: Guidance on AI and data protection
- Organisation/author: Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), UK
- Type: PRIMARY-REG
- URL: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/guidance-on-ai-and-data-protection/
- Published/updated: last updated 2023-03-15 (noted under review) | Accessed: 2026-09-11
- Key finding: Applies data-protection principles to AI — lawfulness, fairness, transparency, accuracy, accountability/governance — including DPIA considerations and safeguards for solely automated decisions (UK GDPR Article 22).
- Relevance: Defines governance information a UK-oriented AI workflow should capture.
- Limitations: Under review pending the Data (Use and Access) Act; overview page rather than full detail.

### EV-005 — ICO: Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)
- Organisation/author: Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), UK
- Type: PRIMARY-REG
- URL: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/data-protection-impact-assessments-dpias/
- Published/updated: as displayed on page | Accessed: 2026-09-11
- Key finding: A DPIA is legally required where processing is "likely to result in high risk." Triggers include innovative technologies, large-scale processing, automated decision-making with significant effects, and processing of vulnerable individuals. DPIA follows a structured describe-assess-mitigate-document process.
- Relevance: Establishes when the workflow must prompt a DPIA and what it must record.
- Limitations: Trigger list is criteria-based and requires case-by-case judgement; exact page date to confirm.

### EV-006 — UK DSIT: A pro-innovation approach to AI regulation (white paper)
- Organisation/author: Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT), UK
- Type: PRIMARY-REG
- URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-regulation-a-pro-innovation-approach/white-paper
- Published: 2023-03-29 | Accessed: 2026-09-11
- Key finding: Five cross-sectoral principles — (1) safety, security and robustness; (2) appropriate transparency and explainability; (3) fairness; (4) accountability and governance; (5) contestability and redress. Applied flexibly by existing regulators.
- Relevance: A recognised UK framing for what "responsible AI" fields the workflow should capture.
- Limitations: Principles-based and non-statutory; later policy has continued to evolve.

### EV-007 — IIBA: How AI enhances business analysis without replacing judgment
- Organisation/author: IIBA (Stacie A. Benson)
- Type: PROF-BODY
- URL: https://www.iiba.org/business-analysis-blogs/how-ai-enhances-business-analysis-without-replacing-professional-judgment/
- Published: 2026-03-11 | Accessed: 2026-09-11
- Key finding: AI accelerates synthesis and administrative tasks (theming, summarising, formatting); BA judgement remains human — technique selection, quality validation against consistent themes, and accountability. "AI should be treated as an assistant, not an authority."
- Relevance: Independent professional-body support for the AI-assists / BA-decides control model.
- Limitations: Opinion/blog format from one practitioner, not empirical study.

### EV-008 — ITONICS: AI innovation management tools (competitor landscape)
- Organisation/author: ITONICS (vendor)
- Type: VENDOR (claims)
- URL: https://www.itonics-innovation.com/blog/ai-innovation-management-tools
- Published: undated blog | Accessed: 2026-09-11
- Key finding (vendor claim): Innovation-management tooling spans foresight, ideation and portfolio management. Named platforms include ITONICS, HYPE, Qmarkets, Brightidea, Planview IdeaPlace, Ideanote, Agorize, rready. Vendor argues general AI (e.g. ChatGPT) lacks organisational context and structured workflow.
- Relevance: Maps the adjacent innovation-management category for comparison.
- Limitations: Vendor marketing; platform strengths are self-described, not independently verified.

### EV-009 — Gartner: AI use-case portfolio / prioritisation offerings
- Organisation/author: Gartner (analyst)
- Type: ANALYST
- URL: https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6663634 ; https://www.gartner.com/en/products/ai-use-case-insights

### EV-010 — Practitioner AI use-case prioritisation frameworks (value–feasibility)
- Organisation/author: Multiple practitioner sources
- Type: SECONDARY
- URL: e.g. https://enterpriseaiexecutive.ai/p/12-ai-use-case-prioritization-frameworks ; https://iternal.ai/ai-use-case-identification
- Published: various | Accessed: 2026-09-11
- Key finding: A value-vs-feasibility (impact/effort) scoring matrix is a common, widely-repeated approach to selecting AI use cases.
- Relevance: Confirms prioritisation practice is mature and commoditised; differentiation must be elsewhere.
- Limitations: Secondary/practitioner blogs of variable rigour; convergent but not authoritative individually.
